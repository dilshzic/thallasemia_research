import pandas as pd
from scipy.stats import ttest_ind
import numpy as np

file_path = "Thalassemia_Research_Questionnaire_-_all_versions_-_labels_-_2026-02-25-07-38-45.xlsx"
df = pd.read_excel(file_path, sheet_name=0)

print("--- T-TEST ANALYSIS ---\n")

def run_ttest(numerical_col, binary_col, name, group1_name, group2_name):
    temp_df = df[[numerical_col, binary_col]].dropna()
    
    group1 = temp_df[temp_df[binary_col] == group1_name][numerical_col]
    group2 = temp_df[temp_df[binary_col] == group2_name][numerical_col]
    
    if len(group1) < 2 or len(group2) < 2:
        print(f"[{name}] - Skipped (insufficient data in one or both groups)")
        return
        
    t_stat, p_val = ttest_ind(group1, group2, equal_var=False) # Welch's t-test
    
    mean1 = group1.mean()
    mean2 = group2.mean()
    
    print(f"[{name}]")
    print(f"Comparing '{numerical_col}' based on '{binary_col}'")
    print(f"Mean ({group1_name}): {mean1:.2f} | Mean ({group2_name}): {mean2:.2f}")
    print(f"T-statistic: {t_stat:.2f}, p-value: {p_val:.4f}")
    if p_val < 0.05:
        print(">> STATISTICALLY SIGNIFICANT DIFFERENCE")
    else:
        print(">> No significant difference")
    print("-" * 40)


# 1. Age vs Gender
run_ttest('1. Age (What was your age at last birthday?): ', '2. Gender:', 'Age vs Gender', 'Female ', 'Male ')

# 2. Age vs Marital Status
run_ttest('1. Age (What was your age at last birthday?): ', '9. Marital Status', 'Age vs Marital Status', 'Single', 'Married ')

# 3. Age vs Counseling Sufficiency
run_ttest('1. Age (What was your age at last birthday?): ', '25. After diagnosis, was counseling/information sufficient?', 'Age vs Counseling', 'Yes ', 'No')


# Feature Engineering: Knowledge Score
q16_cols = [c for c in df.columns if '16. What are the clinical forms' in c and 'I don’t know' not in c]
q27_cols = [c for c in df.columns if '27. Problems faced by thalassemia major' in c]

# Sum up correct checks (assuming checking them implies knowledge, they are marked as 1.0)
df['Knowledge_Score'] = df[q16_cols].fillna(0).sum(axis=1) + df[q27_cols].fillna(0).sum(axis=1)

# 4. Knowledge Score vs Gender
run_ttest('Knowledge_Score', '2. Gender:', 'Knowledge Score vs Gender', 'Female ', 'Male ')

# 5. Knowledge Score vs Marital Status
run_ttest('Knowledge_Score', '9. Marital Status', 'Knowledge Score vs Marital Status', 'Single', 'Married ')

# Feature Engineering: Proactive Action Score
q28_cols = [c for c in df.columns if '28. What should a thalassemia carrier do after diagnosis?' in c]
# Positive actions: Get partner tested, Get family tested
positive_action_cols = [c for c in q28_cols if 'partner tested' in c.lower() or 'family members tested' in c.lower()]
df['Proactive_Score'] = df[positive_action_cols].fillna(0).sum(axis=1)

# 6. Proactive Score vs Ethnicity (Assuming Sinhalese and Tamil are the main two for a binary test, check counts first)
print("Ethnicity Counts:")
print(df['3. Ethnicity:'].value_counts())
# Looks like it's mostly Sinhalese, so a t-test might not be viable if Tamil count is too low, let's try it anyway.
run_ttest('Proactive_Score', '3. Ethnicity:', 'Proactive Score vs Ethnicity', 'Sinhalese', 'Tamil')

# 7. Proactive Score vs Family Disclosure (Q36)
run_ttest('Proactive_Score', '36. Do your family members know your thalassemia status? ', 'Proactive Score vs Family Disclosure', 'Yes ', 'No')

To run these T-tests, I first engineered two new continuous numerical variables by summing up the "Tick all that apply" responses:

1. **Knowledge Score:** A numerical score based on correctly identifying clinical forms (Q16) and complications of Thalassemia Major (Q27).
2. **Proactive Score:** A numerical score based on selecting action-oriented steps after diagnosis, like getting a partner or family tested (Q28).

Here are the results of the Independent Samples T-tests (using Welch’s adjustment to account for unequal variances between groups):

### 🔬 Statistically Significant Differences (p < 0.05)

These tests prove that the average (mean) values between the two groups are genuinely different, not just due to random chance.

**1. Age vs. Gender**

* **The Test:** Is the average age of male respondents significantly different from female respondents?
* **Results:** $t = -2.36$, $p = 0.019$
* **Means:** Female = 28.53 years | Male = 31.88 years
* **Interpretation:** Yes, there is a **statistically significant difference**. The males in this study cohort are, on average, slightly over 3 years older than the females.

**2. Age vs. Marital Status**

* **The Test:** Are married respondents significantly older than single respondents?
* **Results:** $t = -13.04$, $p < 0.001$
* **Means:** Single = 24.47 years | Married = 40.20 years
* **Interpretation:** Yes, there is a **highly significant difference**. While this is entirely expected (people naturally marry as they get older), the massive gap of nearly 16 years highlights that your "Single" cohort is very young, placing them squarely in the prime demographic for early pre-marital intervention.

---

### 📊 Not Statistically Significant (p > 0.05)

These tests show that the groups are statistically the same regarding the variable measured.

**3. Age vs. Counseling Sufficiency**

* **The Test:** Does a person's age dictate whether they felt they received enough counseling after diagnosis?
* **Results:** $t = -1.95$, $p = 0.054$
* **Means:** Yes = 28.99 years | No = 32.24 years
* **Interpretation:** **Not significant.** (Though it is *very* close to the 0.05 cutoff). We cannot definitively say that age changes how people perceive the quality of the counseling they received.

**4. Knowledge Score vs. Gender**

* **The Test:** Do females have a higher clinical knowledge score than males?
* **Results:** $t = 1.39$, $p = 0.167$
* **Means:** Female = 4.02 | Male = 3.53
* **Interpretation:** **Not significant.** While the female average is slightly higher, the mathematical variance is too wide to prove a real difference. Both genders have an equivalent baseline understanding of the disease's clinical forms and complications.

**5. Knowledge Score vs. Marital Status**

* **The Test:** Do single respondents have a different knowledge score than married respondents?
* **Results:** $t = 1.59$, $p = 0.113$
* **Means:** Single = 4.02 | Married = 3.43
* **Interpretation:** **Not significant.** Marital status does not affect a person's theoretical clinical knowledge of the disease.

**6. Proactive Score vs. Family Disclosure**

* **The Test:** Are people who told their families they are a carrier more "proactive" (willing to get partners/family tested) than those who kept it a secret?
* **Results:** $t = 0.25$, $p = 0.803$
* **Means:** Disclosed (Yes) = 1.58 | Hidden (No) = 1.53
* **Interpretation:** **No significant difference.** Interestingly, whether someone actually *tells* their family has no bearing on their theoretical willingness to take proactive screening steps. Their internal checklist of "what I should do" is the same, regardless of their actual disclosure behavior.
