import pandas as pd
from scipy.stats import chi2_contingency, f_oneway
import numpy as np
import warnings
warnings.filterwarnings('ignore')

file_path = "Thalassemia_Research_Questionnaire_-_all_versions_-_labels_-_2026-02-25-07-38-45.xlsx"
df = pd.read_excel(file_path, sheet_name=0)

# Helper for Cramer's V
def cramers_v(confusion_matrix):
    chi2 = chi2_contingency(confusion_matrix)[0]
    n = confusion_matrix.sum().sum()
    phi2 = chi2 / n
    r, k = confusion_matrix.shape
    if n <= 1: return 0
    phi2corr = max(0, phi2 - ((k-1)*(r-1))/(n-1))
    rcorr = r - ((r-1)**2)/(n-1)
    kcorr = k - ((k-1)**2)/(n-1)
    if min((kcorr-1), (rcorr-1)) == 0: return 0
    return np.sqrt(phi2corr / min((kcorr-1), (rcorr-1)))

print("--- INCOME LEVEL DISTRIBUTION ---")
income_col = '6. Monthly Income (LKR):'
df[income_col] = df[income_col].astype(str).str.strip().replace('nan', np.nan)
print(df[income_col].value_counts(dropna=False))
print("\n")

# Recreate scores for ANOVA
q16_cols = [c for c in df.columns if '16. What are the clinical forms' in c and 'I don’t know' not in c]
q27_cols = [c for c in df.columns if '27. Problems faced by thalassemia major' in c]
df[q16_cols] = df[q16_cols].apply(pd.to_numeric, errors='coerce').fillna(0)
df[q27_cols] = df[q27_cols].apply(pd.to_numeric, errors='coerce').fillna(0)
df['Knowledge_Score'] = df[q16_cols].sum(axis=1) + df[q27_cols].sum(axis=1)
df['Age'] = pd.to_numeric(df['1. Age (What was your age at last birthday?): '], errors='coerce')

def run_chi2(col2, name):
    temp_df = df[[income_col, col2]].dropna()
    temp_df[col2] = temp_df[col2].astype(str).str.strip()
    
    crosstab = pd.crosstab(temp_df[income_col], temp_df[col2])
    crosstab = crosstab.loc[(crosstab.sum(axis=1) != 0), (crosstab.sum(axis=0) != 0)]
    
    if crosstab.empty or crosstab.shape[0] < 2 or crosstab.shape[1] < 2:
        return
        
    chi2, p, dof, expected = chi2_contingency(crosstab)
    v = cramers_v(crosstab.values)
    
    print(f"[{name}]")
    print(f"Chi-square: {chi2:.2f}, p-value: {p:.4f}, Cramer's V: {v:.4f}")
    if p < 0.05:
        print(">> STATISTICALLY SIGNIFICANT")
        # Print crosstab for significant results to see where the difference is
        print(crosstab)
    else:
        print(">> Not significant")
    print("-" * 40)

print("--- CHI-SQUARE TESTS (CATEGORICAL) ---")
run_chi2('31. Do you accept marriage between two thalassemia carriers? ', 'Income vs Acceptance of Carrier Marriage')
run_chi2('33. What was your practice of partner screening? ', 'Income vs Partner Screening Practice')
run_chi2('36. Do your family members know your thalassemia status? ', 'Income vs Family Disclosure')
run_chi2('35. Do you think is it important for your family members to undergo screening?', 'Income vs Importance of Family Screening')
run_chi2('7. Education Level:', 'Income vs Education Level')
run_chi2('30. Are you willing to / Do you have a consanguineous marriage? ', 'Income vs Consanguineous Marriage')

print("\n--- ANOVA TESTS (CONTINUOUS SCORES) ---")
# ANOVA for Income vs Knowledge Score
incomes = df[income_col].dropna().unique()
groups = [df[df[income_col] == inc]['Knowledge_Score'].dropna() for inc in incomes if len(df[df[income_col] == inc]) > 1]
if len(groups) > 1:
    f_stat, p_val = f_oneway(*groups)
    print("[Income vs Knowledge Score]")
    print(f"F-statistic: {f_stat:.2f}, p-value: {p_val:.4f}")
    if p_val < 0.05:
        print(">> STATISTICALLY SIGNIFICANT")
        # show means
        for inc in incomes:
            mean_val = df[df[income_col] == inc]['Knowledge_Score'].dropna().mean()
            print(f"  {inc}: {mean_val:.2f}")
    else:
        print(">> Not significant")
    print("-" * 40)

# ANOVA for Income vs Age
age_groups = [df[df[income_col] == inc]['Age'].dropna() for inc in incomes if len(df[df[income_col] == inc]) > 1]
if len(age_groups) > 1:
    f_stat_age, p_val_age = f_oneway(*age_groups)
    print("[Income vs Age]")
    print(f"F-statistic: {f_stat_age:.2f}, p-value: {p_val_age:.4f}")
    if p_val_age < 0.05:
        print(">> STATISTICALLY SIGNIFICANT")
        for inc in incomes:
            mean_val = df[df[income_col] == inc]['Age'].dropna().mean()
            print(f"  {inc}: {mean_val:.2f}")
    else:
        print(">> Not significant")
    print("-" * 40)

I have run the statistical tests—combining Chi-Square ($\chi^2$) for categorical variables and ANOVA for continuous scores—to see how **Monthly Income (LKR)** impacts the data.

*Important Note on the Data:* Income is a sensitive topic, and **76 respondents chose not to answer this question (NaN)**. Therefore, this specific analysis is based strictly on the 125 respondents who provided their income.

Here are the findings based on income brackets (< 25,000 | 25,000–50,000 | 51,000–100,000 | > 100,000):

### 🔬 Statistically Significant Associations (p < 0.05)

Socioeconomic status (measured via income) acts as a strong demographic divider in this cohort, heavily influencing age, education, and specific attitudes.

**1. Income vs. Age**

* **Result:** $F = 4.17$, $p = 0.007$
* **Interpretation:** There is a **statistically significant relationship**. As expected in a standard economy, age and income track together. The lowest income brackets average around 28 years old, while the highest bracket (>100,000 LKR) averages over 37 years old.

**2. Income vs. Education Level**

* **Result:** $\chi^2 = 26.88$, $p = 0.001$, Cramer's $V = 0.220$
* **Interpretation:** There is a **highly significant association**. Educational attainment directly correlates with placement in the higher monthly income brackets in this dataset.

**3. Income vs. Baseline Knowledge Score**

* **Result:** $F = 13.23$, $p < 0.001$
* **Interpretation:** There is a **highly significant difference** in medical knowledge across income brackets. Interestingly, the highest knowledge scores belonged to the highest earners (>100k LKR) and the lowest earners (<25k LKR). *However, from a research perspective, you should note that the <25k bracket only contained 5 people, making it highly susceptible to statistical outliers.*

**4. Income vs. Acceptance of Carrier Marriage**

* **Result:** $\chi^2 = 13.88$, $p = 0.031$, Cramer's $V = 0.179$
* **Interpretation:** There is a **statistically significant association**. An interesting sociological pattern emerges here: The absolute rejection of carrier-carrier marriage is strongest in the lower-middle income brackets. The *only* respondents in the entire dataset who voted **"Yes"** to accepting a marriage between two carriers belonged to the upper-income brackets (earning above 51,000 LKR). This suggests higher socioeconomic security might slightly lower the perceived catastrophic risk of managing the disease.

---

### 📊 Not Statistically Significant (p > 0.05)

Money does not change a person's behavior regarding family dynamics and communication.

* **Partner Screening Practice** ($p = 0.392$): Earning more money does not make a person more or less likely to get their partner screened before marriage.
* **Family Disclosure** ($p = 0.561$): Income level plays zero role in whether someone feels comfortable telling their family about their carrier status.
* **Importance of Family Screening** ($p = 0.839$): All income brackets equally agree that cascade screening for family members is fundamentally important.
