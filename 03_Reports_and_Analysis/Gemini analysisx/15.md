# Helper for Cramer's V
def cramers_v(confusion_matrix):
    chi2 = chi2_contingency(confusion_matrix)[0]
    n = confusion_matrix.sum().sum()
    phi2 = chi2 / n
    r, k = confusion_matrix.shape
    if n <= 1: return 0
    phi2corr = max(0, phi2 - ((k-1)*(r-1))/(n-1))
    rcorr = r - ((r-1)**2)/(n-1)
    kcorr = k - ((k-1)**2)/(n-1)
    if min((kcorr-1), (rcorr-1)) == 0: return 0
    return np.sqrt(phi2corr / min((kcorr-1), (rcorr-1)))

When analyzing the dataset, there is a stark, quantifiable contrast between "Surface Knowledge" (basic facts that are easily memorized from public health posters) and "Deep Knowledge" (understanding the actual pathophysiology and clinical realities of the disease).

Here is the breakdown of the variance between what is obvious to the public versus where their knowledge fails:

### 🟢 Surface Knowledge (The "Obvious")

The public health campaigns have been highly successful at establishing baseline facts. The vast majority of respondents understand the fundamental nature of the disease.

* **Is it a blood-related disease?** 90.0% correctly said "Yes".
* **Is it hereditary?** 89.9% correctly understand it is passed from generation to generation.
* **Is it preventable?** 75.1% correctly recognize that the spread can be prevented.

### 🔴 Deep Knowledge (The "Clinical Reality")

The knowledge gap widens dramatically when respondents are tested on the *actual clinical complications* of Thalassemia Major. While they know it requires blood, they do not understand the systemic toll the disease takes on the body.

When asked to identify the specific problems faced by patients:

* **The Obvious Symptom:** 84.1% know it requires "Regular blood transfusions for life."
* **The Clinical Blindspots:**
* Only **31.8%** recognize it causes severe *Fatigue*.
* Only **26.4%** understand the risk of *Iron overload affecting the heart and liver* (hemochromatosis secondary to transfusions).
* Only **23.9%** know it causes *Delayed growth and puberty*.
* Only **20.4%** realize it causes *Bone deformities* (like extramedullary hematopoiesis/chipmunk facies).
* Only **7.5%** are aware of the endocrine risks (*Diabetes, infertility, hormonal issues*).



### 📉 The Ultimate Knowledge Gap: Curability

The most critical failure in deep knowledge surrounds the prognosis of the disease.

When asked, *"Can thalassemia major be cured?"*:

* **47.8%** believe it simply "Cannot be cured" (fatalistic view).
* **33.3%** admit they "Don't know."
* Only **14.9%** possess the deep medical knowledge to correctly answer that it is *"Very difficult (e.g., bone marrow transplant)."*

**Conclusion for your Research:** The data proves that current education strategies in Sri Lanka are successfully communicating *what* thalassemia is, but failing to communicate *why* it is so devastating. Because people don't understand the severe, systemic complications (iron overload, bone deformities) or the extreme difficulty of a cure (bone marrow transplants), they may not feel the necessary urgency to screen themselves or their partners.
