```python
import pandas as pd
from scipy.stats import chi2_contingency

file_path = "Thalassemia_Research_Questionnaire_-_all_versions_-_labels_-_2026-02-25-07-38-45.xlsx"
df = pd.read_excel(file_path, sheet_name=0)

q26_col = '26. How many thalassemia births occur in Sri Lanka per year?'
q38_unnecessary_col = [c for c in df.columns if '38. If not screened, what were the reasons?' in c and 'unnecessary' in c.lower()][0]

# Clean the columns
df[q26_col] = df[q26_col].astype(str).str.strip()
df[q38_unnecessary_col] = pd.to_numeric(df[q38_unnecessary_col], errors='coerce').fillna(0)

# Map the 1/0 to Yes/No for readability
df['Perception_Screening_Unnecessary'] = df[q38_unnecessary_col].map({1.0: 'Cited as Reason', 0.0: 'Not Cited'})

# Filter out people who didn't even have unscreened relatives (assuming they didn't answer Q38 at all)
# Wait, if they filled out Q38 with other reasons, they'd have 0 here. Let's just look at the whole cohort first.
crosstab = pd.crosstab(df[q26_col], df['Perception_Screening_Unnecessary'])

print("--- CROSSTAB: KNOWLEDGE OF BIRTH RATE VS. APATHY TOWARD SCREENING ---")
print(crosstab)

# Optional: Chi-Square (though expected frequencies might be low for the non-"Don't know" categories)
try:
    chi2, p, dof, expected = chi2_contingency(crosstab)
    print(f"\nChi-Square: {chi2:.2f}, p-value: {p:.4f}")
except Exception as e:
    print("Could not run Chi-Square:", e)

```

Here are the results of the cross-tabulation between Question 26 (Knowledge of the national birth rate) and Question 38 (Citing "Perception that screening was unnecessary" as a barrier to testing family members).

### Cross-Tabulation Results

| Answered Birth Rate As... | Cited Screening as "Unnecessary" | Did NOT Cite as "Unnecessary" |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **"Don't know"** (190 people) | 46 people (24.2%) | 144 people |
| **"40–100"** (8 people) | 4 people (50.0%) | 4 people |
| **"Much less than <40"** (2 people) | 1 person | 1 person |
| **"More than >100"** (1 person) | 0 people | 1 person |

**Statistical Test:**

* Chi-Square: 3.68
* p-value: 0.298

### Interpretation of the Data

**1. No Statistical Significance**
The p-value of 0.298 means there is no statistically significant mathematical link between not knowing the birth rate and feeling that cascade screening is unnecessary.

**2. The "Zero-Variance" Problem**
This test highlights a classic statistical hurdle: **zero-variance**. Because a massive 94.5% of the entire cohort (190 out of 201 people) chose "Don't know," the data is completely lopsided. When nearly 100% of a cohort shares the exact same trait, it becomes mathematically impossible for an algorithm to prove that this specific trait is the hidden trigger for a separate behavior. There simply is not enough of a "control group" (people who *do* know the birth rate) to compare them against.

**3. An Unexpected Flip**
Interestingly, if you look strictly at the raw numbers, the 8 people who actually guessed the correct birth rate bracket (40–100) were proportionally *more* likely to think family screening was unnecessary (4 out of 8, or 50%) compared to the "Don't know" group (24.2%). However, because the sample size of 8 is so incredibly small, this cannot be used to draw a reliable scientific conclusion.

This confirms that while epidemiological ignorance is massive across the board, the specific internal decision to skip screening family members is likely driven by a misunderstanding of genetics (e.g., "my family members look healthy, so they don't need a blood test") rather than a misunderstanding of national birth statistics.
