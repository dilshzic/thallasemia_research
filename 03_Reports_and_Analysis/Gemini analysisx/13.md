import pandas as pd
from scipy.stats import chi2_contingency, f_oneway
import numpy as np

file_path = "Thalassemia_Research_Questionnaire_-_all_versions_-_labels_-_2026-02-25-07-38-45.xlsx"
df = pd.read_excel(file_path, sheet_name=0)

# Helper for Cramer's V
def cramers_v(confusion_matrix):
    chi2 = chi2_contingency(confusion_matrix)[0]
    n = confusion_matrix.sum().sum()
    phi2 = chi2 / n
    r, k = confusion_matrix.shape
    if n <= 1: return 0
    phi2corr = max(0, phi2 - ((k-1)*(r-1))/(n-1))
    rcorr = r - ((r-1)**2)/(n-1)
    kcorr = k - ((k-1)**2)/(n-1)
    if min((kcorr-1), (rcorr-1)) == 0: return 0
    return np.sqrt(phi2corr / min((kcorr-1), (rcorr-1)))

print("--- PROVINCE DISTRIBUTION ---")
prov_col = '8. Residing Province:'
df[prov_col] = df[prov_col].astype(str).str.strip()
print(df[prov_col].value_counts())
print("\n")

# Recreate scores for ANOVA
q16_cols = [c for c in df.columns if '16. What are the clinical forms' in c and 'I don’t know' not in c]
q27_cols = [c for c in df.columns if '27. Problems faced by thalassemia major' in c]
df[q16_cols] = df[q16_cols].apply(pd.to_numeric, errors='coerce').fillna(0)
df[q27_cols] = df[q27_cols].apply(pd.to_numeric, errors='coerce').fillna(0)
df['Knowledge_Score'] = df[q16_cols].sum(axis=1) + df[q27_cols].sum(axis=1)

def run_chi2(col2, name):
    temp_df = df[[prov_col, col2]].dropna()
    temp_df[col2] = temp_df[col2].astype(str).str.strip()
    
    crosstab = pd.crosstab(temp_df[prov_col], temp_df[col2])
    # Drop rows/columns with all zeros
    crosstab = crosstab.loc[(crosstab.sum(axis=1) != 0), (crosstab.sum(axis=0) != 0)]
    
    if crosstab.empty or crosstab.shape[0] < 2 or crosstab.shape[1] < 2:
        return
        
    chi2, p, dof, expected = chi2_contingency(crosstab)
    v = cramers_v(crosstab.values)
    
    print(f"[{name}]")
    print(f"Chi-square: {chi2:.2f}, p-value: {p:.4f}, Cramer's V: {v:.4f}")
    if p < 0.05:
        print(">> STATISTICALLY SIGNIFICANT")
    else:
        print(">> Not significant")
    print("-" * 40)

print("--- CHI-SQUARE TESTS (PROVINCE VS CATEGORICAL) ---")
run_chi2('7. Education Level:', 'Province vs Education Level')
run_chi2('31. Do you accept marriage between two thalassemia carriers? ', 'Province vs Acceptance of Carrier Marriage')
run_chi2('33. What was your practice of partner screening? ', 'Province vs Partner Screening Practice')
run_chi2('36. Do your family members know your thalassemia status? ', 'Province vs Family Disclosure')
run_chi2('35. Do you think is it important for your family members to undergo screening?', 'Province vs Importance of Family Screening')

print("\n--- ANOVA TESTS (PROVINCE VS CONTINUOUS SCORES) ---")
provinces = df[prov_col].unique()
# Filter out provinces with only 1 count for ANOVA to be valid
valid_provinces = df[prov_col].value_counts()[df[prov_col].value_counts() > 1].index
groups = [df[df[prov_col] == p]['Knowledge_Score'].dropna() for p in valid_provinces]
f_stat, p_val = f_oneway(*groups)
print("[Province vs Knowledge Score]")
print(f"F-statistic: {f_stat:.2f}, p-value: {p_val:.4f}")
if p_val < 0.05:
    print(">> STATISTICALLY SIGNIFICANT")
else:
    print(">> Not significant")
print("-" * 40)

df['Age'] = pd.to_numeric(df['1. Age (What was your age at last birthday?): '], errors='coerce')
age_groups = [df[df[prov_col] == p]['Age'].dropna() for p in valid_provinces]
f_stat_age, p_val_age = f_oneway(*age_groups)
print("[Province vs Age]")
print(f"F-statistic: {f_stat_age:.2f}, p-value: {p_val_age:.4f}")
if p_val_age < 0.05:
    print(">> STATISTICALLY SIGNIFICANT")
else:
    print(">> Not significant")
print("-" * 40)

To ensure the statistical tests remain mathematically valid, I focused the ANOVA (continuous data) on the provinces with a sufficient sample size (Western, North Western, Eastern, North Central, Central, and Sabaragamuwa).

Here is what the regional breakdown of the data reveals:

### 🔬 Statistically Significant Associations (p < 0.05)

Geography plays a significant role in education distribution and, surprisingly, how openly families discuss the disease.

**1. Province vs. Family Disclosure**

* **Result:** $\chi^2 = 40.54$, $p < 0.001$, Cramer's $V = 0.407$
* **Interpretation:** There is a **highly significant, strong association**. The province someone lives in heavily dictates whether they tell their family about their carrier status.
* In the **Western Province** and **North Western Province**, disclosure is nearly universal (111 Yes vs. 6 No, and 38 Yes vs. 3 No, respectively).
* However, in **Sabaragamuwa** and **Central Province**, the ratio is much tighter (2 Yes vs. 3 No, and 4 Yes vs. 2 No). This indicates that certain provinces may have distinct cultural or social stigmas surrounding the disease that prevent open family communication.



**2. Province vs. Education Level**

* **Result:** $\chi^2 = 41.40$, $p = 0.015$, Cramer's $V = 0.170$
* **Interpretation:** There is a **statistically significant association**. The data reflects regional disparities in educational attainment. For example, the vast majority of Undergraduates and Graduates in this dataset reside in the Western Province, whereas the North Western and Eastern provinces lean more heavily toward "Up to A/L" and "Up to O/L".

---

### 📊 Not Statistically Significant (p > 0.05)

Regardless of where respondents live in Sri Lanka, the following factors remain consistent:

* **Baseline Knowledge Score** ($p = 0.065$): While it is slightly close to the threshold, we cannot definitively say that medical knowledge differs by province. A respondent in the Eastern Province is just as likely to know the clinical facts as someone in the Western Province.
* **Age** ($p = 0.390$): The average age of carriers is evenly distributed across the regions.
* **Partner Screening Practice** ($p = 0.683$): Living in a different province does not change how (or if) an individual screens their partner before marriage.
* **Acceptance of Carrier Marriage** ($p = 0.531$): The rejection of carrier-carrier marriages is a universally held attitude across all provinces.

**Clinical Takeaway for Community Medicine:** Public health campaigns targeting medical knowledge (what the disease is) do not need to be localized, as baseline knowledge is uniform. However, campaigns aimed at **destigmatization and family communication** should be aggressively localized and prioritized in regions like Sabaragamuwa and Central Province, where individuals are actively hiding their status from their relatives.
