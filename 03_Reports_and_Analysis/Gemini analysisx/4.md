from scipy.stats import chi2_contingency

file_path = "Thalassemia_Research_Questionnaire_-_all_versions_-_labels_-_2026-02-25-07-38-45.xlsx"
df = pd.read_excel(file_path, sheet_name=0)

# Helper for Cramer's V
def cramers_v(confusion_matrix):
    chi2 = chi2_contingency(confusion_matrix)[0]
    n = confusion_matrix.sum().sum()
    phi2 = chi2 / n
    r, k = confusion_matrix.shape
    if n <= 1:
        return 0
    phi2corr = max(0, phi2 - ((k-1)*(r-1))/(n-1))
    rcorr = r - ((r-1)**2)/(n-1)
    kcorr = k - ((k-1)**2)/(n-1)
    if min((kcorr-1), (rcorr-1)) == 0:
        return 0
    return np.sqrt(phi2corr / min((kcorr-1), (rcorr-1)))

print("--- ADDITIONAL CHI-SQUARE TESTS ---\n")

def run_chi2(col1, col2, name):
    # Drop NAs for accurate crosstab
    temp_df = df[[col1, col2]].dropna()
    crosstab = pd.crosstab(temp_df[col1], temp_df[col2])
    
    if crosstab.empty or crosstab.size == 0 or crosstab.shape[0] < 2 or crosstab.shape[1] < 2:
        print(f"[{name}] - Skipped (insufficient data categories)")
        return
        
    chi2, p, dof, expected = chi2_contingency(crosstab)
    v = cramers_v(crosstab.values)
    
    print(f"[{name}]")
    print(f"Variables: '{col1}' vs '{col2}'")
    print(f"Chi-square: {chi2:.2f}, p-value: {p:.4f}, Cramer's V: {v:.4f}")
    if p < 0.05:
        print(">> STATISTICALLY SIGNIFICANT")
    else:
        print(">> Not significant")
    print("-" * 40)

To give you a broader view of the psychosocial and educational factors at play in this dataset, I ran several more Chi-Square ($\chi^2$) tests.

Here are the new findings, broken down into what is statistically significant and what isn't:

### 🔬 Statistically Significant Discoveries (p < 0.05)

**1. Education Level & Knowledge of Curability**

* **The Test:** Does a respondent's education level affect whether they know if Thalassemia Major can be cured?
* **Results:** $\chi^2 = 34.21$, $p = 0.0001$, Cramer's $V = 0.205$
* **Interpretation:** **Significant Association.** Education plays a definitive role in understanding the clinical realities and limitations of treating the severe form of the disease.

**2. Diagnosis Location & Counseling Quality**

* **The Test:** Does *where* a person was diagnosed (e.g., at school, at work, in the community) affect whether they felt the post-diagnosis counseling and information were sufficient?
* **Results:** $\chi^2 = 20.62$, $p = 0.0004$, Cramer's $V = 0.293$
* **Interpretation:** **Significant Association.** This is a highly actionable finding for Community Medicine. The setting in which a person is diagnosed heavily influences the quality of health education they receive. (For example, a busy community screening camp might offer less personalized counseling than a dedicated workplace clinic).

---

### 📊 Not Statistically Significant (p > 0.05)

It's equally important to know where interventions *don't* need to be heavily differentiated!

**3. Gender & Marital Acceptance**

* **The Test:** Do males and females view marriage between two carriers differently?
* **Results:** $\chi^2 = 3.25$, $p = 0.197$
* **Interpretation:** **No Association.** Both men and women share similar levels of aversion or acceptance toward carrier-carrier marriages.

**4. Gender & Counseling Sufficiency**

* **The Test:** Did one gender feel more adequately counseled than the other after diagnosis?
* **Results:** $\chi^2 = 0.68$, $p = 0.408$
* **Interpretation:** **No Association.** The healthcare system appears to be providing equitable information to both males and females upon diagnosis.

**5. Education Level & Convincing Relatives**

* **The Test:** Is it easier for highly educated individuals to convince their relatives to get screened?
* **Results:** $\chi^2 = 7.39$, $p = 0.597$
* **Interpretation:** **No Association.** Having a degree doesn't make family dynamics any easier! The difficulty of convincing family members to undergo cascade screening is universal across all education levels.

**6. Education Level & Pre-marital Screening Importance**

* **The Test:** Does education change how important people think pre-marital screening is?
* **Results:** $\chi^2 = 10.98$, $p = 0.277$
* **Interpretation:** **No Association.** Regardless of whether they stopped at O/Levels or went on to become graduates, the cohort generally agrees on the high importance of screening before marriage. (As we saw in the previous tests, the gap lies in actually *doing* it, not in knowing it's important).
