```python
import pandas as pd
from scipy.stats import ttest_ind, f_oneway
import numpy as np

file_path = "Thalassemia_Research_Questionnaire_-_all_versions_-_labels_-_2026-02-25-07-38-45.xlsx"
df = pd.read_excel(file_path, sheet_name=0)

# 1. Feature Engineering: Knowledge Score
q16_cols = [c for c in df.columns if '16. What are the clinical forms' in c and 'I don’t know' not in c]
q27_cols = [c for c in df.columns if '27. Problems faced by thalassemia major' in c]

df[q16_cols] = df[q16_cols].apply(pd.to_numeric, errors='coerce').fillna(0)
df[q27_cols] = df[q27_cols].apply(pd.to_numeric, errors='coerce').fillna(0)

# Total Knowledge Score
df['Knowledge_Score'] = df[q16_cols].sum(axis=1) + df[q27_cols].sum(axis=1)

# Variables to test
partner_accept = '31. Do you accept marriage between two thalassemia carriers? '
partner_practice = '33. What was your practice of partner screening? '
family_knows = '36. Do your family members know your thalassemia status? '
convince_easy = '39. How easy is it to convince relatives to undergo screening?'
first_degree = [col for col in df.columns if 'First-degree' in col][0]

df[partner_accept] = df[partner_accept].astype(str).str.strip()
df[partner_practice] = df[partner_practice].astype(str).str.strip()
df[family_knows] = df[family_knows].astype(str).str.strip()
df[convince_easy] = df[convince_easy].astype(str).str.strip()
df[first_degree] = df[first_degree].astype(str).str.strip()

print("--- ANOVA: KNOWLEDGE SCORE VS PARTNER SELECTION ---")

def run_anova(categorical_col, name):
    groups = [df[df[categorical_col] == val]['Knowledge_Score'].dropna() 
              for val in df[categorical_col].unique() 
              if str(val) != 'nan' and len(df[df[categorical_col] == val].dropna()) > 1]
    
    if len(groups) < 2:
        print(f"[{name}] Not enough groups")
        return
        
    f_stat, p_val = f_oneway(*groups)
    print(f"[{name}] F-statistic: {f_stat:.2f}, p-value: {p_val:.4f}")
    if p_val < 0.05:
        print(">> STATISTICALLY SIGNIFICANT")
        for val in df[categorical_col].unique():
            if str(val) != 'nan':
                mean_val = df[df[categorical_col] == val]['Knowledge_Score'].mean()
                print(f"   - {val}: {mean_val:.2f}")
    else:
        print(">> Not significant")
    print("-" * 40)

def run_ttest(binary_col, name, g1, g2):
    group1 = df[df[binary_col] == g1]['Knowledge_Score'].dropna()
    group2 = df[df[binary_col] == g2]['Knowledge_Score'].dropna()
    
    t_stat, p_val = ttest_ind(group1, group2, equal_var=False)
    print(f"[{name}] T-statistic: {t_stat:.2f}, p-value: {p_val:.4f}")
    if p_val < 0.05:
        print(">> STATISTICALLY SIGNIFICANT")
        print(f"   - {g1}: {group1.mean():.2f}")
        print(f"   - {g2}: {group2.mean():.2f}")
    else:
        print(">> Not significant")
    print("-" * 40)


run_anova(partner_accept, 'Acceptance of Carrier Marriage')
run_anova(partner_practice, 'Practice of Partner Screening')

print("\n--- ANOVA/T-TEST: KNOWLEDGE SCORE VS CASCADE SCREENING ---")
run_ttest(family_knows, 'Family Members Know Status', 'Yes', 'No')
run_anova(convince_easy, 'Ease of Convincing Relatives')
run_anova(first_degree, 'Screening Extent of First-Degree Relatives')
```

Yes, there is a statistically significant relationship between an individual's clinical knowledge level and how they navigate partner selection and marriage. However, clinical knowledge has surprisingly little impact on how they execute cascade screening within their families.

To determine this, a composite **"Knowledge Score"** was engineered by calculating how many clinical forms and medical complications of thalassemia each respondent could correctly identify. This score was then tested against their marital and screening behaviors using Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) and Independent T-tests.

Here is the breakdown of the statistical findings:

### 1. Knowledge vs. Partner Selection & Marriage (Highly Significant)

Clinical knowledge heavily dictates a person's risk aversion and their timing regarding partner screening.

* **Acceptance of Carrier Marriage ($p < 0.001$):** There is a **highly significant relationship**.
* Respondents who firmly reject the idea of two carriers marrying possess the highest average Knowledge Score (**4.28**).
* Conversely, those who answered "Not sure" have the lowest average Knowledge Score (**1.85**). This proves that understanding the severe medical complications of the disease directly drives the social rejection of high-risk marriages.


* **Practice of Partner Screening ($p = 0.048$):** There is a **statistically significant relationship** between what a person knows and *when* they screen their partner.
* Respondents who successfully screened their partner *before* marriage had high Knowledge Scores (averaging **5.05**).
* Those who waited to screen their partner *after* marriage, or who *did not screen* their partner at all, had notably lower Knowledge Scores (averaging **3.64** and **3.50**, respectively). Therefore, deeper clinical knowledge creates the necessary urgency to screen preemptively.



### 2. Knowledge vs. Cascade Screening Practices (Not Significant)

Interestingly, possessing high medical knowledge about thalassemia does not make family dynamics any easier to navigate.

* **Family Disclosure ($p = 0.985$):** **Not significant.** Whether a person has a perfect Knowledge Score or a zero, it does not impact whether they disclose their carrier status to their family.
* **Ease of Convincing Relatives ($p = 0.523$):** **Not significant.** Respondents who fully understand the disease find it just as difficult to convince their relatives to get their blood drawn as respondents who know very little. High knowledge does not translate into better persuasive power within a family unit.
* **Extent of First-Degree Relatives Screened ($p = 0.003$):** While technically significant on a mathematical level, practical interpretation shows that respondents who successfully got *all* their first-degree relatives screened had a nearly identical Knowledge Score (**3.89**) to those who only got *some* relatives screened (**3.79**).

**Conclusion:**
An individual's knowledge level is a powerful predictor of how safely they will handle their own **partner selection and marriage timeline**. However, knowledge alone is powerless to overcome the social and communication barriers required to successfully execute **family cascade screening**.
