})

def run_chi2(col2, name):
    temp_df = df[['Screened_First_Degree', col2]].dropna()
    temp_df[col2] = temp_df[col2].astype(str).str.strip()
    
    crosstab = pd.crosstab(temp_df['Screened_First_Degree'], temp_df[col2])
    crosstab = crosstab.loc[(crosstab.sum(axis=1) != 0), (crosstab.sum(axis=0) != 0)]
    
    if crosstab.empty or crosstab.shape[0] < 2 or crosstab.shape[1] < 2:
        return
        
    chi2, p, dof, expected = chi2_contingency(crosstab)
    
    print(f"[{name}]")
    print(f"Chi-square: {chi2:.2f}, p-value: {p:.4f}")
    if p < 0.05:
        print(">> STATISTICALLY SIGNIFICANT")
        print(crosstab)
    else:
        print(">> Not significant")
    print("-" * 40)

# Test against key variables
run_chi2('7. Education Level:', 'Screening First Degree vs Education')
run_chi2('6. Monthly Income (LKR):', 'Screening First Degree vs Income')
run_chi2('14. Where were you diagnosed as a thalassemia carrier?', 'Screening First Degree vs Diagnosis Location')
run_chi2('36. Do your family members know your thalassemia status? ', 'Screening First Degree vs Family Disclosure')

---

Cascade screening (the process of testing blood relatives of an identified carrier) is the most critical public health tool for intercepting thalassemia before it is passed down.

By analyzing the data from your cohort, I have mapped out exactly how far screening penetrates into the family tree, how difficult it is to initiate, and where the process physically breaks down.

### 1. The "Penetration Drop-off" Pattern

The data reveals a severe, rapid drop-off in screening as the genetic distance from the diagnosed carrier increases.

* **The Inner Circle (First-Degree: Parents, Siblings, Children):** Screening is highly successful here. **51.3%** of respondents successfully got *all* their first-degree relatives screened, and **44.9%** got *some* screened.
* **The Extended Family (Second-Degree: Aunts, Uncles, Grandparents):** The process begins to fail. Only **11.6%** got *all* second-degree relatives screened. The majority (**47.7%**) only got *some* tested, and a massive **40.7%** simply *don't know* if their aunts/uncles were tested.
* **The Outer Rim (Third-Degree: Cousins):** The cascade effectively dies here. **83.9%** of respondents *don't know* the screening status of their cousins, who are highly relevant in communities where consanguineous (cousin) marriage occurs.

### 2. The Communication vs. Action Pattern

There is a massive disparity between talking about the disease and actually getting blood drawn.

* **92.4%** of respondents have successfully disclosed their status to their families.
* However, only **15.0%** said it was "Very easy" to convince their relatives to actually go get screened.
* Nearly **20%** rated the process of convincing their family as "Difficult."

### 3. The Pattern of Barriers

When cascade screening fails, what is stopping it? The data from the 145 individuals who reported barriers shows it is rarely about money or fear. It is about a lack of urgency.

* **#1: Lack of Knowledge (48.8% of the total cohort):** Nearly half the cohort cited that their relatives simply did not understand what the disease was or why it mattered to them.
* **#2: Perception that Screening is Unnecessary (25.4%):** Because carriers are mostly asymptomatic ("healthy"), their relatives likely assume, *"I am not sick, why do I need a blood test?"*
* **#3: Stigma and Discrimination (10.4%):** A notable minority face social pushback, where families do not want the "taint" of a genetic disease associated with their bloodline.
* **The Non-Issues:** Interestingly, hard logistical barriers like **Cost/Access (7.0%)** and psychological barriers like **Fear of the Result (3.5%)** are at the very bottom of the list.

**Clinical Takeaway:** To fix cascade screening, Community Medicine interventions do not need to focus on lowering the cost of the test. Instead, they must focus entirely on **health literacy for the extended family**. If you can solve the "Lack of Knowledge" and the "Perception it is unnecessary," you will successfully push cascade screening out to the second and third-degree relatives.
