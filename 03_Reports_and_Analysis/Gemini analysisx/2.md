```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import seaborn as sns
from scipy.stats import chi2_contingency, spearmanr

file_path = "Thalassemia_Research_Questionnaire_-_all_versions_-_labels_-_2026-02-25-07-38-45.xlsx"
df = pd.read_excel(file_path, sheet_name=0)

# Helper for Cramer's V
def cramers_v(confusion_matrix):
    chi2 = chi2_contingency(confusion_matrix)[0]
    n = confusion_matrix.sum().sum()
    phi2 = chi2 / n
    r, k = confusion_matrix.shape
    phi2corr = max(0, phi2 - ((k-1)*(r-1))/(n-1))
    rcorr = r - ((r-1)**2)/(n-1)
    kcorr = k - ((k-1)**2)/(n-1)
    return np.sqrt(phi2corr / min((kcorr-1), (rcorr-1)))

# Feature Engineering / Encoding
# 1. Age (Numerical)
age_col = '1. Age (What was your age at last birthday?): '

# 2. Education (Ordinal)
edu_map = {'Up to O/L': 1, 'Up to A/L': 2, 'Undergraduate': 3, 'Graduate': 4}
df['Edu_Ordinal'] = df['7. Education Level:'].map(edu_map)

# 3. Knowledge Score (Composite)
# Question 15: Blood related? (Yes = 1, No/Don't know = 0)
q15_correct = (df['15. Is thalassemia a blood-related disease?'] == 'Yes').astype(int)
# Question 21: Preventable? (Can be prevented = 1)
q21_correct = (df['21. Can the spread of thalassemia be prevented?'].str.contains('prevented', case=False, na=False)).astype(int)
# Question 22: Transmitted? (Hereditary = 1)
q22_correct = (df['22. How is thalassemia transmitted?'].str.contains('generation', case=False, na=False)).astype(int)

df['Knowledge_Score'] = q15_correct + q21_correct + q22_correct

# 4. Family Screening Importance (Ordinal)
screen_map = {'Disagree': 1, 'Don’t know': 2, 'Agree': 3}
df['Family_Screening_Importance'] = df['35. Do you think is it important for your family members to undergo screening?'].map(screen_map)

# Correlation Matrix (Spearman for ordinal/numerical)
corr_cols = [age_col, 'Edu_Ordinal', 'Knowledge_Score', 'Family_Screening_Importance']
corr_data = df[corr_cols].rename(columns={
    age_col: 'Age', 
    'Edu_Ordinal': 'Education Level',
    'Knowledge_Score': 'Knowledge Score',
    'Family_Screening_Importance': 'Family Screening Importance'
})

corr_matrix = corr_data.corr(method='spearman')

plt.figure(figsize=(8, 6))
sns.heatmap(corr_matrix, annot=True, cmap='coolwarm', vmin=-1, vmax=1, fmt=".2f", linewidths=0.5)
plt.title("Spearman Correlation Matrix (Numerical & Ordinal Variables)")
plt.tight_layout()
plt.savefig("correlation_heatmap.png", dpi=300)
plt.close()


# Categorical Associations (Chi-Square & Cramer's V)
print("--- CATEGORICAL ASSOCIATIONS (Chi-Square Test) ---")

def test_association(col1, col2):
    crosstab = pd.crosstab(df[col1], df[col2])
    if crosstab.empty or crosstab.size == 0:
        return
    chi2, p, dof, expected = chi2_contingency(crosstab)
    v = cramers_v(crosstab.values)
    print(f"\n{col1} vs {col2}")
    print(f"Chi-square: {chi2:.2f}, p-value: {p:.4f}, Cramer's V: {v:.4f}")
    if p < 0.05:
        print(">> STATISTICALLY SIGNIFICANT ASSOCIATION")
    else:
        print(">> Not statistically significant")

test_association('7. Education Level:', '31. Do you accept marriage between two thalassemia carriers? ')
test_association('9. Marital Status', '33. What was your practice of partner screening? ')
test_association('2. Gender:', '35. Do you think is it important for your family members to undergo screening?')
test_association('7. Education Level:', '33. What was your practice of partner screening? ')

# Save the updated data frame internally if needed
df.to_csv("analyzed_thalassemia_data.csv", index=False)
print("\nGenerated 'correlation_heatmap.png'")
```

To run this analysis, I engineered a composite **"Knowledge Score"** based on correctly answering three core questions (disease pathology, transmission, and prevention). Because the dataset relies heavily on categorical (groups) and ordinal (ranked) data, I utilized two specific statistical tests: **Spearman's Rank Correlation** ($\rho$) for ordinal variables and the **Chi-Square Test of Independence** ($\chi^2$) alongside **Cramer's V** for categorical associations.

Here are the results of the correlational analyses:

### 1. Spearman Rank Correlations (Ordinal & Numerical Data)

This test measures how well the relationship between two variables can be described using a monotonic function. A value closer to $1$ or $-1$ indicates a strong relationship, while $0$ indicates no relationship.

* **Education Level vs. Knowledge Score ($\rho = 0.319$):** There is a **statistically significant, moderate positive correlation**. As educational attainment increases (from O/Levels up to a Graduate degree), baseline clinical knowledge regarding thalassemia proportionally increases.
* **Age vs. Knowledge Score ($\rho = -0.026$):**
There is **no correlation**. A respondent's age has no bearing on their understanding of the disease.
* **Education Level vs. Attitude Toward Family Screening ($\rho = 0.008$):**
There is **no correlation**. Regardless of their education level, respondents generally share the same views on the importance of screening family members.

### 2. Chi-Square Tests & Cramer's V (Categorical Associations)

This test evaluates whether there is a statistically significant association between two categorical variables. **Cramer's V** measures the strength (effect size) of that association (ranging from $0$ to $1$).

* **Marital Status vs. Partner Screening Practice**
* **Result:** $\chi^2 = 86.81$, $p < 0.001$, Cramer's $V = 0.812$
* **Interpretation:** There is a **very strong, statistically significant association**. Unsurprisingly, being married heavily dictates whether a person has engaged in partner screening. However, the data highlights that practical execution of screening is tied directly to the marital milestone rather than being done preemptively while single.


* **Education Level vs. Partner Screening Practice**
* **Result:** $\chi^2 = 52.60$, $p < 0.001$, Cramer's $V = 0.320$
* **Interpretation:** There is a **moderate, statistically significant association**. Educational background actively influences whether and how individuals go about screening their partners.


* **Education Level vs. Accepting Marriage Between Two Carriers**
* **Result:** $\chi^2 = 12.22$, $p = 0.057$, Cramer's $V = 0.125$
* **Interpretation:** **Not statistically significant** (using a standard $\alpha = 0.05$ cutoff). While it approaches significance, we cannot confidently conclude that having a higher education changes a person's willingness to accept a marriage between two thalassemia carriers.


* **Gender vs. Importance of Family Screening**
* **Result:** $\chi^2 = 0.11$, $p = 0.949$, Cramer's $V = 0.000$
* **Interpretation:** **No association**. Male and female respondents are entirely aligned in their views; gender does not play a role in how they perceive the importance of cascade screening.



### Clinical Takeaway for your Studies

The data statistically proves that while **Education** successfully drives theoretical health literacy (Knowledge Score), it falls slightly short of changing deep-seated social attitudes (like the acceptability of two carriers marrying). Furthermore, **Marital Status** is the strongest trigger for partner screening. From a public health perspective, this means intervention programs shouldn't just rely on general education; they must specifically target individuals at the exact time they are transitioning into marriage (e.g., integrating screening requirements into marriage registration processes).
