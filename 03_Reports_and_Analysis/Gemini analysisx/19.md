### Why it feels like an "Unfair" Question (The Practical Angle)

From a purely practical standpoint, it is a slightly flawed expectation. Real health literacy is not about memorizing textbook epidemiological incidence rates. A person does not need to know the exact statistical birth rate to successfully get their partner screened and prevent the disease in their own family. Because the failure rate is 95%, this variable is completely useless for measuring a person's *individual clinical knowledge*.

### Why it is a Brilliant "Burden" Indicator (The Community Medicine Angle)

In public health, understanding the *scale* of a problem dictates the community's response.
If people think there are 10,000 births a year, they panic. If they think there are 0, they do not care. By asking this question, researchers are testing **perceived urgency**. The fact that 95% of carriers have no idea how many babies are born with the disease proves that the community is entirely blind to the national economic and healthcare burden of thalassemia.
