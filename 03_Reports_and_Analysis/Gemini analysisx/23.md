import pandas as pd

import numpy as np
import warnings

file_path = "Thalassemia_Research_Questionnaire_-_all_versions_-_labels_-_2026-02-25-07-38-45.xlsx"

# Helper for Cramer's V
def cramers_v(confusion_matrix):
    chi2 = chi2_contingency(confusion_matrix)[0]
    n = confusion_matrix.sum().sum()
    phi2 = chi2 / n
    r, k = confusion_matrix.shape
    if n <= 1: return 0
    phi2corr = max(0, phi2 - ((k-1)*(r-1))/(n-1))
    rcorr = r - ((r-1)**2)/(n-1)
    kcorr = k - ((k-1)**2)/(n-1)
    if min((kcorr-1), (rcorr-1)) == 0: return 0
    return np.sqrt(phi2corr / min((kcorr-1), (rcorr-1)))

print("--- EDUCATION LEVEL DISTRIBUTION ---")
edu_col = '7. Education Level:'
df[edu_col] = df[edu_col].astype(str).str.strip()
print(df[edu_col].value_counts())
print("\n")

# Recreate scores for ANOVA
q16_cols = [c for c in df.columns if '16. What are the clinical forms' in c and 'I don’t know' not in c]
q27_cols = [c for c in df.columns if '27. Problems faced by thalassemia major' in c]
df[q16_cols] = df[q16_cols].apply(pd.to_numeric, errors='coerce').fillna(0)
df[q27_cols] = df[q27_cols].apply(pd.to_numeric, errors='coerce').fillna(0)
df['Knowledge_Score'] = df[q16_cols].sum(axis=1) + df[q27_cols].sum(axis=1)

def run_chi2(col2, name):
    temp_df = df[[edu_col, col2]].dropna()
    temp_df[col2] = temp_df[col2].astype(str).str.strip()
    
    crosstab = pd.crosstab(temp_df[edu_col], temp_df[col2])
    crosstab = crosstab.loc[(crosstab.sum(axis=1) != 0), (crosstab.sum(axis=0) != 0)]
    
    if crosstab.empty or crosstab.shape[0] < 2 or crosstab.shape[1] < 2:
        return
        
    chi2, p, dof, expected = chi2_contingency(crosstab)
    v = cramers_v(crosstab.values)
    
    print(f"[{name}]")
    print(f"Chi-square: {chi2:.2f}, p-value: {p:.4f}, Cramer's V: {v:.4f}")
    if p < 0.05:
        print(">> STATISTICALLY SIGNIFICANT")
    else:
        print(">> Not significant")
    print("-" * 40)

print("--- CHI-SQUARE TESTS (EDUCATION VS CATEGORICAL) ---")
run_chi2('31. Do you accept marriage between two thalassemia carriers? ', 'Education vs Acceptance of Carrier Marriage')
run_chi2('33. What was your practice of partner screening? ', 'Education vs Partner Screening Practice')

if p_val < 0.05:
    print(">> STATISTICALLY SIGNIFICANT")
else:
    print(">> Not significant")
print("-" * 40)

df['Age'] = pd.to_numeric(df['1. Age (What was your age at last birthday?): '], errors='coerce')
age_groups = [df[df[edu_col] == e]['Age'].dropna() for e in educations if len(df[df[edu_col] == e]) > 1]
f_stat_age, p_val_age = f_oneway(*age_groups)
print("[Education vs Age]")
print(f"F-statistic: {f_stat_age:.2f}, p-value: {p_val_age:.4f}")
if p_val_age < 0.05:
    print(">> STATISTICALLY SIGNIFICANT")
else:
    print(">> Not significant")

To answer your question, I ran a series of statistical tests comparing **Education Level** against the demographic, behavioral, and knowledge variables in the dataset.

The cohort is distributed across four educational tiers: **Up to O/L** (38 respondents), **Up to A/L** (83 respondents), **Undergraduates** (43 respondents), and **Graduates** (37 respondents).

Here is what the statistical analysis reveals about the role of education:

### 🔬 Statistically Significant Associations (p < 0.05)

Education level is a massive driver of theoretical clinical knowledge and a major factor in when (or if) people choose to screen their partners.

**1. Education vs. Clinical Knowledge Score**

* **Result:** $F = 17.78$, $p < 0.001$
* **Interpretation:** There is a **highly significant difference** in how well different educational groups understand the specific medical complications of thalassemia.
* **Undergraduates** (5.33) and **Graduates** (5.14) have nearly identical, high knowledge scores.
* **Up to A/L** (3.05) and **Up to O/L** (2.47) have significantly lower scores. This proves that basic secondary education is not successfully teaching the deeper clinical realities of the disease; advanced post-secondary education is required to grasp the systemic complications.



**2. Education vs. Practice of Partner Screening**

* **Result:** $\chi^2 = 52.60$, $p < 0.001$, Cramer's $V = 0.320$
* **Interpretation:** There is a **highly significant, moderate association**. Education dictates the *timing* and execution of partner screening.
* **The Proactive Group:** Graduates were the most likely to get their partner screened *before* marriage (10 out of 25 graduates who answered this question).
* **The Delayed Group:** Those with O/L and A/L education heavily skewed towards screening their partner *after* marriage (27 individuals) or *not screening them at all* (20 individuals).



**3. Education vs. Age**

* **Result:** $F = 7.52$, $p < 0.001$
* **Interpretation:** There is a **statistically significant difference**. Undergraduates are the youngest group (average age 24.5 years), while the O/L group is the oldest (average age 34.0 years).

---

### 📊 Not Statistically Significant (p > 0.05)

Higher education does not change deep-seated social attitudes or family dynamics.

* **Acceptance of Carrier Marriage ($p = 0.057$):** While it approaches significance, we cannot mathematically prove that Graduates are more or less accepting of carrier-carrier marriages than those with O/Levels. All groups heavily reject the idea.
* **Family Disclosure ($p = 0.160$):** Having a university degree does not make a person more likely to tell their family they are a carrier. The communication barriers transcend education.
* **Importance of Family Screening ($p = 0.899$):** Whether a respondent stopped at O/Levels or went on to become a Graduate, everyone universally agrees that cascade screening is important.
* **Importance of Pre-marital Screening ($p = 0.277$):** Similarly, everyone *knows* that screening before marriage is important; the gap (as seen in finding #2 above) is that only the highly educated individuals actually *execute* it before tying the knot.

**Clinical Takeaway:** Education works beautifully for improving theoretical knowledge, but standard secondary education (O/L and A/L) is failing to trigger *proactive behavior*. Since the O/L and A/L groups are significantly more likely to wait until *after* marriage to screen their partners, Community Medicine interventions should specifically target these demographics with actionable, rather than purely educational, mandates.
