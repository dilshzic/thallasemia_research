## Abstract
This report presents an empirical evaluation of clinical and genetic knowledge among 201 confirmed $\beta$-thalassemia carriers identified through school, workplace, and community screening initiatives across Sri Lanka. Knowledge was quantified using an unweighted linear scoring model (maximum score = 20 points), eliminating artificial difficulty weights to ensure reproducibility and alignment with medical education standards. Participant performance was evaluated using a dual-threshold methodology: (a) an empirical cohort mean split ($M = 11.72, SD = 3.41$) and (b) a modified Bloom's criterion (Good $\ge 80\%$, Moderate $60\text{--}79\%$, Poor $< 60\%$). The cohort demonstrated strong surface awareness regarding the inherited nature of the condition ($86.6\%$) and the necessity of lifelong transfusions for thalassemia major ($83.1\%$). However, severe deficiencies emerged in critical quantitative risk literacy: only $41.3\%$ correctly identified the $25\%$ offspring recurrence risk for two carrier parents, and only $37.8\%$ recognized the clinical limitations of curative bone marrow transplantation. These findings highlight that routine carrier screening fails to confer adequate genetic literacy in the absence of structured post-test genetic counseling.

## 1. Introduction and Theoretical Framework
$\beta$-Thalassemia poses a major public health challenge in Sri Lanka, where the carrier frequency is estimated at $9.9\%$ for $\alpha$-thalassemia trait and $2.5\%$ to $5.0\%$ for $\beta$-thalassemia trait, with high regional concentrations in the North Western, North Central, and Western provinces. Prevention programs rely primarily on voluntary premarital screening and cascade family testing. However, the success of prevention is fundamentally mediated by carriers' comprehension of their genetic status and reproductive risk.

This investigation assesses the baseline clinical and genetic literacy of diagnosed carriers to identify systemic educational gaps and provide an objective scoring rubric for clinical trial and educational intervention design.

## 2. Methodology and Scoring Design

### 2.1 Instrument and Domain Structure
The survey instrument evaluated five fundamental clinical sub-domains:
1. **General Etiology and Pathology:** Classification as an inherited disorder of hemoglobin synthesis (Q14, Q21).
2. **Clinical Forms and Phenotypic Spectrum:** Differentiation between asymptomatic carrier state (trait), intermedia, and transfusion-dependent thalassemia major (Q15, Q16).
3. **Mendelian Inheritance and Recurrence Risk:** Autosomal recessive transmission mechanics and prospective offspring probability calculation (Q19, Q22, Q23).
4. **Complications and Lifelong Disease Burden:** Transfusion-associated hemosiderosis, cardiac/hepatic failure, growth retardation, and curative limitations (Q18, Q20, Q26).
5. **Epidemiological Context and Preventive Responsibilities:** National birth prevalence, cascade testing, and premarital partner screening (Q25, Q27).

### 2.2 Unweighted Scoring Model vs. Dual Cut-Off System
To provide transparent, non-arbitrary assessment:
* **Unweighted Linear Scoring:** Each validated correct answer received 1.0 point (with partial credit for multi-select questions scaled proportionally), yielding an integer continuous scale from 0 to 20. Artificial $(1-p)$ inverse-frequency weights were rejected to avoid cohort-dependent score instability.
* **Empirical Mean Cut-Off:** Defined by the cohort mean ($M = 11.72$). Participants scoring $\ge 11.72$ are classified as *High Knowledge*; those $< 11.72$ are classified as *Low Knowledge*.
* **Modified Bloom's Criterion:** 
  * **Good Knowledge:** Score $\ge 16$ ($\ge 80.0\%$)
  * **Moderate Knowledge:** Score 12 to 15 ($60.0\%\text{--}79.9\%$)
  * **Poor Knowledge:** Score $\le 11$ ($< 60.0\%$)

## 5. Discussion and Clinical Implications
1. **The Dichotomy of High Awareness vs. Poor Literacy:** While general awareness of thalassemia as a hereditary disorder is high ($86.6\%\text{--}94.5\%$), granular genetic comprehension is critically low. More than half ($58.7\%$) failed to calculate the Mendelian 1-in-4 probability of having an affected child. This "all-or-nothing" belief creates catastrophic relationship cancellations or, conversely, reckless fatalism.
2. **Curability Misconceptions:** Over $44\%$ believed routine medications or routine blood transfusions can permanently cure thalassemia major. Underestimating disease chronicity reduces the perceived necessity of strict premarital carrier screening.
3. **Inadequate Counseling Infrastructure:** With $41.3\%$ of diagnosed carriers reporting inadequate post-test information, diagnostic screening currently acts as a passive label rather than an empowering public health intervention.
